# ICONOCRACIA
## Female allegory and the material practices of legal authority in Brazil, 1822–1922
**Ana Vitória Vanzin Mendes | PPGD/UFSC**  
**Working project brief, 13 November 2026. For Ana's review, not submitted.**

### Research problem
Female figures have long represented Justice, the Republic and the nation. Their prominence can coexist with unequal access to civil and political agency. That contrast is an important starting point, but it does not, on its own, explain how a particular image operated within a legal institution.

ICONOCRACIA examines the documentary relations connecting female allegories to the representation and recognition of legal authority in Brazil between independence and its centenary. It asks who selected these figures, for which objects and institutions, under what production constraints, and with what documented uses. It also asks what changes when an image is considered for an official purpose but never enters circulation.

The central research question is therefore conditional: under what historically demonstrable circumstances did female allegories participate in practices of legal authority, and how can their representational or legitimating functions be distinguished from institutional identification, formal authentication and legal justification?

### Scope and contribution
Brazil, 1822–1922, provides the core of demonstration. Foreign objects and later examples belong to a comparative catalogue whose role must be identified case by case. The circulation of French repertoires is a specific historical problem, not an assumed genealogy. Training, copying, catalogue purchase, commission and reception are different routes that require different sources.

The proposed contribution is a differentiated account of visual and legal operations. An official commission establishes institutional investment in a representation; it does not alone establish public acceptance or the image's role in the reasons for a legal rule. Likewise, a design appearing on money does not by itself explain the conditions of valid issuance. This distinction makes the inquiry specifically relevant to legal history, while retaining dialogue with art history, feminist scholarship and studies of visual culture.

The project does not claim to have discovered the tension between female personification and women's exclusion. Its originality must be demonstrated through precise comparisons with existing scholarship and through the reconstruction of particular legal and institutional relations.

### Materials and method
The research combines object dossiers, legal sources, documented institutional histories and comparative visual analysis. The relevant materials include constitutional imagery, judicial painting, legal-book illustrations and monetary or other official designs. A dossier should identify the object or version reproduced, its maker where established, its date or justified interval, its custodial source, and the evidence concerning commission, manufacture, acquisition, placement, circulation and reception.

Three analytical layers remain distinct: documentary record, theoretical philology and iconological hypothesis. Interpretation is informed by historicism and critical attention to gender and colonial relations. Contemporary concepts are not treated as the language of historical actors.

Ten ordinal indicators remain separate observations, accompanied by verbal description when used. No composite score of authority or purification is proposed. Foundational, normative and military regimes are interpretative categories to be justified within cases. Counter-allegory functions as a critical test, not a fourth regime and not an inherently emancipatory practice. Visual montage generates comparisons and questions; it cannot independently establish historical transmission.

### Representative dossiers and their limits
**Debret and constitutional representation.** The distinction between the 1822 theatre curtain and the later published plate is essential. The dossier asks how constitutional authority could be staged and how allegorical, dynastic, maternal and participatory positions differ. A link to a particular constitutional provision requires documentary support beyond formal resemblance.

**Amoedo and the judicial image.** The female Justice associated with the former Supreme Federal Court offers a test of claims that institutional authority necessarily requires suppression of bodily characteristics. The executed work, preparatory studies and dates require careful differentiation. Spatial interpretation, commission history and judicial reasoning remain distinct evidentiary tasks.

**Visconti and an unexecuted official proposal.** The catalogue entry A813 provides a way to examine the difference between recognition of a design and its implementation. A rewarded proposal that was not executed cannot be treated as an issued object or proof of public reception. The explanation for non-execution remains a separate question.

The Palace bust in Florianópolis and comparisons among legal-book illustrations provide further research leads. Their value depends on resolving the relevant acquisition, attribution and edition questions rather than supplying those relations by inference.

### Current stage and next steps
The project has developed a corpus infrastructure, methodological documents and several case dossiers. A full-project presentation and recent methodological reviews identify both productive distinctions and unresolved evidence. These materials should not be presented as a completed validation of the entire corpus. Counts, edition identities and provenance claims must be tied to dated sources and defined units.

The immediate priorities are to select a small set of demonstrable cases, reconcile the methodological versions used in distribution, and compare the proposed contribution directly with relevant scholarship. Further archival work should target documents capable of changing the interpretation: commissioning correspondence, selection records, acquisition evidence, authorized-design regulations and contemporary responses.

I am seeking scholarly discussion about the project's legal-historical contribution, the choice of cases and an institutional setting in which it could develop with appropriate supervision and funding.
