# Cross-Media Visual Montages in Legal History: A Research Framework for Legal Iconology and Iconocracy

## 1. Theoretical & Epistemological Foundations: Iconocracy, Nocturnal Jurisprudence, and the Panofsky-Warburg Synthesis

### 1.1 Introductory Context & Strategic Importance
The investigation of female state allegories (*Feminilidade de Estado*) within legal history demands a methodological departure from viewing visual artifacts as passive architectural embellishments or illustrative ornaments appended to pre-existing statutory codes. Visual legal history demonstrates that state iconography functions as an active epistemic mechanism of legal authority. State iconocracy (*Iconocracia*)—the institutional regime in which visual figures govern what can be seen and what can only be articulated verbally within a political community—executes selective regimes of visibility, authority, and juridical exclusion. These personifications do not merely reflect legal norms produced elsewhere; they actively perform sovereign authority, establishing visual legitimacy prior to any textual argumentation. Analyzing these images requires interrogating how the hypervisibility of classicized, allegorical female bodies serves as a primary instrument for constructing, exercising, and shielding legal power.

> **STATE ICONOCRACY PROTOCOL**
>
> * **Hypervisible State Allegory:** Invested with sempiternal state *dignitas* (*Marianne*, *Britannia*, *Germania*, *Justitia*, *República*), abstracting sovereignty into an unblemished, immortal body.
> * **Juridical Exclusion & Erasure:** Structural disenfranchisement, civil incapacitation, and archival erasure of concrete historical women and racialized populations.
> * **Epistemic Operator:** The visual allegory executes selective visibility, legitimating institutional power before textual legal acts are read.

### 1.2 The Tripartite Panofsky Method Corrected by Warburgian Pathosformel
To evaluate visual legal sources without sliding into impressionistic interpretation or formalist reduction, this framework establishes a hybrid methodological apparatus. It adapts Erwin Panofsky’s three-tiered iconological trajectory—as adapted specifically for legal sources by Georges Martyn (2020)—and integrates it with Aby Warburg’s concept of *Pathosformel* (surviving gestural affect) and Igor Stramignoni’s "nocturnal jurisprudence of the image."

1. **Pre-Iconographic Description:** The foundational empirical stratum documents observable formal structures, postures, limbs, spatial compositions, and physical attributes (e.g., swords, scales, blindfolds, drapery) without immediately assigning conventional names or symbolic legal meanings.
2. **Iconographic Identification:** The secondary stratum links observed formal features to specific cultural and historical conventions, identifying personifications such as *Justitia*, *Marianne*, *Britannia*, *Germania*, or the Brazilian *República*.
3. **Iconological Interpretation:** The tertiary stratum interrogates what the image reveals about—and actively produces within—the surrounding legal culture, uncovering underlying theological, colonial, biopolitical, and gendered mechanisms.

Panofsky’s third level carries an inherent risk: a tendency toward harmonizing interpretations that resolve visual tensions into a coherent, unified cultural narrative. Aby Warburg’s *Pathosformel* serves as an essential internal corrective to this harmonizing impulse. By tracking emotionally charged gestural survivals (*Nachleben*)—such as bleeding bodies, bound wrists, veiled eyes, or violent repulsions—Warburgian analysis resists artificial interpretive closure. It exposes the emotional friction, bodily trauma, and gestural survivals that official legal iconographies attempt to smooth over.

Complementing this corrective is Igor Stramignoni’s concept of "nocturnal jurisprudence" (*Law and Critique*, 2024). Legal imagery operates not only in the transparent, daytime light of official institutional representation (*obiter depicta*), but also in the shadows of *Nachleben der Antike*. While official legal doctrines function under the transparent "daytime" light of institutional self-justification, the nocturnal image acts as the unburied residue (*resto*) of suppressed state violence that returns to haunt the legal order. Nocturnal jurisprudence attends to residual traumas, structural violence, and excluded voices that formal legal doctrines attempt to bury. The legal image thus becomes a site of return for that which the legal order has suppressed, admitting in visual terms the bodily suffering upon which institutional authority is constructed.

| Methodological Stratum | Analytical Action | Legal-Historical Objective | Epistemological Safeguard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-Iconographic Description** | Cataloging pure forms, gestural postures, physical supports, and literal inscriptions. | Establishing an empirical, verifiable baseline of visual attributes (*capta*). | Prevents premature interpretive leaps and projection of anachronistic legal concepts. |
| **Iconographic Identification** | Matching visual motifs with contemporary historical and legal repertories (Martyn 2020). | Identifying specific legal personifications (*Justitia*, *Têmis*, *República*). | Corrected by the history of types to prevent false symbolic identifications. |
| **Iconological Interpretation** | Interrogating the image as a symptom of underlying politico-legal structures. | Uncovering how visual forms construct and legitimate legal authority. | Corrected by the history of cultural symptoms and critical legal theories. |
| **Warburgian *Pathosformel*** | Isolating affective gestural survivals (*Nachleben*) and bodily friction within the scene. | Locating emotional tension, state violence, and non-harmonized trauma. | Interrupts Panofskian over-harmonization and artificial interpretive closure. |
| **Nocturnal Jurisprudence** | Examining visual residues (*resto*), *obiter depicta*, shadows, and institutional exclusions (Stramignoni 2024). | Exposing the suppressed human cost and structural violence of legal orders. | Counters official institutional self-narratives by surfacing buried legal contradictions. |

### 1.3 The Core Paradox: Hypervisibility vs. Juridical Exclusion
At the center of legal iconology lies a structural paradox defined by the *Contrato Sexual Visual* (Visual Sexual Contract) and the *Contrato Racial Visual* (Visual Racial Contract). Throughout modern legal history, the hypervisibility of idealized, classicized female allegories (*Marianne*, *Britannia*, *Germania*, *Justitia*, *República*) directly coincides with the explicit legal restriction, disenfranchisement, or complete erasure of concrete historical women and racialized populations.

Drawing upon Ernst Kantorowicz’s political theology of *The King's Two Bodies*, state iconocracy abstracts legal sovereignty by duplicating the body of the state. While the mortal, natural bodies of subjects age, suffer, and die, the state constructs a sempiternal *dignitas*—a perpetual, flawless political body. By capitalizing and gendering this abstract body as female, state iconography invests sovereignty with an immortal halo. However, this symbolic elevation operates through a profound asymmetrical abstraction:

* **The Allegorical Female Form:** Invested with supreme, timeless authority, personifying foundational virtues, constitutional unity, and sovereign power.
* **Concrete Historical Women:** Stripped of civil capacity, denied political franchise, subjected to marital power (*potestade marital*), and excluded from holding judicial office or framing legislation.

The female form is mobilized as an empty signifier of a legal order in which real women are forbidden to participate. The Visual Sexual and Racial Contracts operate by classicalizing, whitening, and desexualizing the state allegory, transforming the visual sphere into a mechanism that legitimates the very order that enforces concrete subordination.

## 2. Operationalizing Methodological Protocols: The Post-Composite *Iconometria Jurídica* Workflow

### 2.1 Introductory Context & Strategic Importance
To examine corpora of legal visual artifacts without falling into speculative claims or false quantitative determinism, legal iconology requires a rigorous, non-aggregative coding methodology. Methodological revisions in legal history demonstrate that composite quantitative metrics—such as retired "hardening scores" (*score de endurecimento*) that summed or averaged ordinal ratings into a single index—are scientifically invalid. Aggregating ordinal scores mathematically conflates distinct physical supports, masks qualitative shifts, and creates an illusion of statistical precision that obscures historical causation.

Grounding this workflow in exact administrative snapshots—specifically the September 21, 2026 dataset snapshot (comprising 336 cataloged items and 286 purification-coded rows), the Master Codebook v2.2.1 (July 28, 2026), and the formal decisions of July 28, 2026 (retiring composite scores) and September 24/29, 2026 (permanently removing aggregate fields)—ensures structural integrity. Ordinal indicator scores are descriptive qualitative markers (*capta*), not mathematical vectors. Moving to isolated ordinal indicators paired with mandatory qualitative verbal inventories (*atributos_iconograficos*) preserves scientific auditability, respects the material constraints of different media, insulates legal history from pseudo-scientific indexing, and ensures that visual evidence remains fully auditable.

### 2.2 Corpus Stratification & Catalog Taxonomy
To prevent metadata-only records or exploratory computational outputs from being conflated with human-verified historical evidence, research catalogs must enforce a three-tiered stratification system:

1. **Tier 1: Core Adjudicated Dossiers**
   * *Criteria:* Requires complete human visual inspection, verified physical and historical provenance, fully articulated qualitative verbal inventories (*atributos_iconograficos*), and primary legal source documentation.
   * *Role:* Primary driver for in-depth case studies, legal mediation analyses, and visual montage panels.
   * *Promotion Rule:* Promoted from Tier 2 only after a researcher completes direct human verification of the physical artifact and records the comprehensive verbal inventory.
2. **Tier 2: Visually Inspected Comparanda**
   * *Criteria:* Requires high-resolution visual verification (such as International Image Interoperability Framework / IIIF compliance) and confirmed catalog metadata, but lacks full archival mediation documentation.
   * *Role:* Used for comparative validation, tracking iconographic variations, and supporting structural mapping across jurisdictions.
   * *Promotion Rule:* Promoted from Tier 3 upon direct visual inspection of high-resolution digital reproductions.
3. **Tier 3: Exploratory Metadata-Only Records**
   * *Criteria:* Uninspected entries derived from automated web scraping, computational data collection, or unverified library metadata.
   * *Role:* Retained strictly for catalog historiography, diagnostic critique, and initial corpus mapping.
   * *Restriction:* Strictly forbidden from serving as primary evidence for qualitative legal-historical claims or montage argumentations.

### 2.3 The 10 Ordinal Indicators of *Iconometria*
The *IconoCode* Master Codebook (v2.2.1) evaluates morphological and material features across ten separate ordinal indicators, each measured on an explicit 0–3 scale.

#### Morphological Indicators (Indicators 1–7)
1. **Desincorporação (Disembodiment):**
   * `0`: Naturalistic, fully rendered human body.
   * `1`: Idealized classical body.
   * `2`: Generic or stylized body.
   * `3`: Geometrized, abstract, or disembodied form (e.g., bust, head only, or complete physical absence).
2. **Rigidez Postural (Postural Rigidity):**
   * `0`: Dynamic, fluid movement.
   * `1`: Contained, balanced pose (*contrapposto*).
   * `2`: Rigid, frontal, or static posture.
   * `3`: Total hieratism or complete postural immobility.
3. **Dessexualização (Desexualization):**
   * `0`: Explicit corporal exposure or eroticized bodily markers.
   * `1`: Subtle corporal suggestion beneath drapery.
   * `2`: Fully draped, classicized, and gendered body.
   * `3`: Indeterminate, obscured, or fully desexualized body.
4. **Uniformização Facial (Facial Uniformity):**
   * `0`: Individualized portraiture with distinct human features.
   * `1`: Idealized classical facial features.
   * `2`: Generic, standardized, or repetitive face.
   * `3`: Absence of facial features, veiled face, or blank mask.
5. **Heraldicização (Heraldicization):**
   * `0`: Attributes integrated naturally into a narrative scene.
   * `1`: Attributes actively held or wielded by the figure.
   * `2`: Attributes detached or highlighted as symbolic accents.
   * `3`: Isolated emblem or purely heraldic symbol without human agency.
6. **Enquadramento Arquitetônico (Architectural Framing):**
   * `0`: Open, unconstrained landscape or natural space.
   * `1`: Discrete background elements.
   * `2`: Dominant architectural frame or structural border.
   * `3`: Complete absorption into architectural relief, niche, or portal.
7. **Apagamento Narrativo (Narrative Erasure):**
   * `0`: Fully articulated narrative scene with multiple interacting figures.
   * `1`: Suggested narrative context with secondary figures or actions.
   * `2`: Isolated figure with minor contextual vestiges.
   * `3`: Total isolation of the figure on a neutral, decontextualized background.

#### Material Indicators (Indicators 8–10)
8. **Monocromatização (Monochromatization):**
   * `0`: Full naturalistic polychromy.
   * `1`: Reduced or controlled color palette.
   * `2`: Bichromy or strict chromatic restriction.
   * `3`: Complete monochrome (e.g., single-ink print, metal coin, unpainted stone).
9. **Serialidade (Seriality):**
   * `0`: Unique, single-instance work of art (e.g., manuscript painting, unique canvas).
   * `1`: Limited edition print run or fine art series.
   * `2`: Medium-scale publication or institutional print.
   * `3`: Mass industrial reproduction (e.g., coins, postage stamps, mass legal press).
10. **Inscrição Estatal (State Inscription):**
    * `0`: Complete absence of official state markings or text.
    * `1`: Documented official state commission.
    * `2`: Inclusion of official state heraldry, seals, or emblems.
    * `3`: Direct integration into state legal instruments (e.g., constitutional charters, judicial seals, legal tenders).

#### Methodological Imperative: Non-Aggregation
Calculating sums, averages, or composite metrics across these indicators is strictly forbidden. Mathematical aggregation hides qualitative shifts; for instance, a score of `3` in *Monocromatização* on a metallic coin reflects an inherent material constraint, whereas a `3` in *Apagamento Narrativo* on an oil painting represents a deliberate compositional choice. Aggregating these values distorts the material realities of distinct physical supports and obscures the specific mechanics of legal iconography.

### 2.4 The Verbal Inventory Protocol (*atributos_iconograficos*) and Capta Framework
To ensure that ordinal indicators do not degenerate into reductive quantitative abstraction, every coded item must be accompanied by a detailed qualitative verbal inventory (*atributos_iconograficos*). Drawing on Johanna Drucker’s epistemological framework (*Digital Humanities Quarterly*, 2011), data must be understood as *capta*—information that is constructed, situated, and interpreted rather than given as an objective fact.

The verbal inventory protocol requires researchers to record observed visual evidence, physical support conditions, lighting, state of conservation, and historical documentation. Crucially, the protocol enforces a **Split-Variable Qualifier** for framing (Indicator 6) and chromaticity (Indicator 8), explicitly distinguishing:

* **Inherent/Technical (*inerente ao suporte*):** Physical framing or chromatic constraints imposed by the medium itself (e.g., the circular metal rim of a minted coin or the natural monochrome of carved marble).
* **Deliberate/Compositional (*imposto na composição*):** Structural framing or color restrictions intentionally introduced by the artist or commissioning state entity to discipline the figure (e.g., an engraved stone arch surrounding *Justitia* to constrain her posture within a judicial frontispiece).

## 3. Cross-Media Montage Architecture: The *Atlas Iconocrático* Protocol

### 3.1 Introductory Context & Strategic Importance
Visual montage serves as a non-linear analytical engine for legal history. Drawing theoretical inspiration from Aby Warburg’s *Atlas Mnemosyne* and Georges Didi-Huberman’s visual epistemic theory (*Atlas ou le gai savoir inquiet*, 2011), the *Atlas Iconocrático* moves beyond passive chronological galleries. By arranging disparate visual artifacts into spatial montages, researchers can reveal structural continuities, sudden gestural resurgences (*Nachleben*), institutional shifts in state authority, and counter-hegemonic subversions that linear text narratives obscure.

### 3.2 Declared Epistemic Status Grammar for Visual Connectors
A critical danger in visual montage is that readers may misinterpret spatial proximity as evidence of linear historical derivation or Eurocentric diffusion. To ensure evidentiary transparency and prevent false causal claims, all visual pairings within the panels of the *Atlas Iconocrático* must explicitly display a **Declared Epistemic Status Connector**:

> ### DECLARED EPISTEMIC STATUS GRAMMAR
>
> 1. **Solid Line ($\longrightarrow$) — Documented Historical Transmission:**
>    Used *only* when direct archival evidence substantiates a historical linkage between two visual artifacts (e.g., commission contracts, engraver reference models, state purchase receipts, or explicit legislative directives).
>
> 2. **Dashed Line ($\dashrightarrow$) — Formal / Gestural Analogy:**
>    Indicates a shared *Pathosformel* or morphological resonance operating across time or space *without* direct archival proof of contact. This prevents panel arrangements from implying false linear derivations or Eurocentric lineages.
>
> 3. **Jagged / Tension Line ($\rightsquigarrow$) — Satirical Inversion / Counter-Allegory / Legal Contradiction:**
>    Marks an explicit conflict, counter-hegemonic subversion, or structural contradiction (e.g., an official civic allegory paired with a satirical cartoon depicting the state as diseased, or paired with a statute restricting women's civil capacity).

### 3.3 The Eight Thematic Panel Typologies
The *Atlas Iconocrático* is organized across eight structural panel typologies, each designed to execute a specific legal-historical argument:

1. **Cross-Media Compression & Expansion Panel:** Tracks how core attributes (e.g., swords, scales, Phrygian caps, blindfolds) migrate, shed, or transform when moving between monumental architecture (*verticalidade monumental*) and handheld everyday objects like coins and postage stamps (*ubiquidade íntima*).
2. **Bifocal Dossier Panel (*Acoplamento Imagem-Norma*):** Directly couples official visual artifacts (frontispieces, constitutional covers, legal seals) with contemporaneous statutory texts, court rulings, or parliamentary debates to analyze temporal friction between visual hypervisibility and legal restriction.
3. **Foundational-Sacrificial Regimes Panel:** Maps visual representations of state origin points, examining how violence and sacrifice are allegorized during constitutional foundings.
4. **Normative-Juridical Regimes Panel:** Examines the visual codification of everyday legal administration, courtrooms, and administrative hierarchies.
5. **Militar-Imperial Regimes Panel:** Analyzes the transformation of civic allegories into armed, helmeted sentinels during imperial expansion or war.
6. **Satirical Counter-Allegory Panel (*Contra-alegoria*):** Assembles subversive caricatures and press cartoons that deform state iconography to contest political power.
7. **Racialized Reproductive & Emancipation Panel:** Interrogates visual depictions of state emancipation (e.g., abolitionist medals, imperial decrees) against racialized labor and reproductive legal frameworks.
8. **Transnational Transfer & Local Adaptation Panel:** Tracks the international circulation of allegorical models (e.g., French *Marianne* translated into the Brazilian *República*) to highlight local divergences and stylistic adaptations.

## 4. Physical Support & Scale Controls: Materiality vs. Morphology

### 4.1 Introductory Context & Strategic Importance
A central methodological error in visual legal history is analyzing visual iconography without controlling for material support. Reproducing a four-meter courtroom relief and a two-centimeter silver coin at the exact same size on a printed page distorts their historical and sociopolitical function. Comparative visual analysis must systematically account for physical scale, medium constraints, and spatial context to prevent false morphological assumptions.

### 4.2 Comparative Analysis of Material Supports

| Material Support | Spatial Experience & Circulation | Morphological Constraints | Impact on Visual Gender Dynamics | Dominant Iconocratic Regime |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Coins & Postage Stamps** | *Ubiquidade íntima* (Intimate Ubiquity); handheld, personal, highly mobile daily circulation. | Extreme spatial restriction; circular rim; high relief or fine engraving; monochrome metal/paper. | Highly compressed body; frequent reduction to profile bust; extreme facial uniformity. | *Normativo* / *Fundacional* |
| **Monumental Architecture & Sculpture** | *Verticalidade monumental* (Monumental Verticality); fixed public sites; viewed from below (*in situ*). | Architectural integration; massive scale; weather-resistant stone/bronze; fixed lighting. | Hyper-visible, classicized, heroicized female body; rigid posture disciplining the viewer. | *Fundacional* / *Militar* |
| **Legal Paratexts & Constitutional Charters** | *Acoplamento Imagem-Norma*; book-bound; framing legal codes, read at close distance. | Rectangular paper page; engraved line art or lithography; bound directly with text. | Allegory acts as a visual shield/framing device for state norms; abstracting legal authority. | *Normativo-Jurídico* |
| **Satirical Press & Illustrated Periodicals** | *Contra-alegoria*; mass print circulation; ephemeral; dynamic newsstand consumption. | Fast production; woodcut or lithograph; flexible framing; integrated caricatural text. | Deformed, vulnerable, or prostituted female body used to critique political corruption. | *Contra-alegoria* |

### 4.3 Scale-Restitution & Viewpoint Annotation Protocols
To counter scale distortions in print and digital displays, montages must incorporate two mandatory visual safeguards:

1. **1:1 Scale Silhouette / Hand Scale Icon:** Every item reproduced in an Atlas panel must feature an adjacent visual scale indicator—such as a standardized human silhouette or hand icon—that visually communicates the actual physical dimensions of the original artifact.
2. **Viewing Context Badge System:** Catalog entries and visual montages must display explicit standardized contextual badges:
   * `[In Situ / Architectural]`: Indicates an artifact viewed from below in a public building or courtroom.
   * `[Palm / Intimate]`: Indicates an object inspected up close in the hand.
   * `[Paratextual / Text-Bound]`: Indicates an image encountered directly alongside legal text.

These safeguards prevent researchers from mistakenly assuming that identical iconographic motifs execute the same sociopolitical function across radically different physical media.

## 5. Archival Silences, Critical Historiography, and Counter-Archives

### 5.1 Introductory Context & Strategic Importance
Legal archives are not neutral repositories of historical facts; they are products of institutional power, state selection, and structural violence. Drawing on the critical historiographical insights of Saidiya Hartman (*Venus in Two Acts*, 2008), Marisa Fuentes (*Dispossessed Lives*, 2016), and Griselda Pollock, a legal iconology framework must analyze erased subjects, suppressed commissions, and non-extant visual records without resorting to speculative fabrication. Visual legal history must make institutional silences legible while respecting the limits of available empirical evidence.

### 5.2 Methodological Taxonomy of Archival Gaps
To prevent imprecise claims about missing visual material, researchers must distinguish between three distinct modes of archival absence:

1. **Catalogic Lacunae:** Technical, temporary gaps in data acquisition, digitization, or unindexed physical archives (e.g., an unphotographed item in a museum storage room).
2. **Documentary Erasure / Archival Silence:** The systematic, historical omission of subjugated populations—such as enslaved Black women or indigenous communities—from official legal records and state iconography, reflecting their active legal disenfranchisement.
3. **Figurative Absence:** The deliberate omission or removal of an expected allegorical figure from a specific historical commission, visual series, or public monument where its presence was legally or conventionally anticipated.

#### Diagnostic Case: Unresolved Catalog Discrepancy in Record US-020 (*Looking Backward*)
A crucial application of this taxonomy occurs in record US-020 (Joseph Keppler’s 1893 print *Looking Backward*). Methodological audit reveals that record US-020 represents an **unresolved catalog discrepancy**: the canonical ledger incorrectly marked *Columbia* as *observed* in the quantitative fields, whereas the qualitative textual summary declared her *absent* from Keppler's satire. Without direct visual verification of the primary physical print, US-020 cannot be asserted as a proven historical omission. Instead, it serves as the primary diagnostic case demonstrating why visual legal history requires an explicit protocol to manage catalogic uncertainty and unresolved verification states without forcing artificial interpretive closure or fabricating artist intent.

### 5.3 The "Shadow Frame" Protocol for Absent and Unresolved Cases
To visually render archival silences and catalogic discrepancies without fabricating evidence, the *Atlas Iconocrático* operationalizes the **Shadow Frame (Reserved Blank Frame)** protocol:

> ### SHADOW FRAME PROTOCOL (RESERVED BLANK FRAME)
>
> * **Catalog ID:** US-020 (*Looking Backward*, 1893)
> * **Epistemic Status:** Unresolved Catalog Discrepancy / Pending Primary Verification
> * **Operational Function:** Reserves an explicit blank, shadowed frame within panel layouts when visual verification is incomplete or when historical documentation proves an intended allegory was suppressed.
> * **Historiographical Value:** Visually performs feminist critical historiography (Hartman/Fuentes/Pollock), making institutional erasures, legal silences, and catalog uncertainties legible without resorting to speculative closure or unverified attribution.

When a visual commission was canceled, a record destroyed, or a verification state remains unresolved, the montage layout inserts an explicitly labeled blank frame bounded by shadowed lines. Rather than filling gaps with speculative imagery, the Shadow Frame renders institutional erasures and unresolved verification states legible as structural features of the legal archive.

## 6. Comparative Empirical Case Studies: Validating the Framework Across Legal Regimes

### 6.1 Introductory Context & Strategic Importance
To prove its empirical validity, this research framework is applied to three distinct historical stress points: early modern European cartographic statecraft, classical legal iconology under judicial challenge in the twentieth century, and state-building during Brazilian constitutional transitions. These case studies demonstrate how the framework integrates thematic analysis, material controls, and critical historiography across varying legal regimes.

### 6.2 Case Study I: *Europa Regina* & *Europa Lamentans* (1534–1598) — The Wounded Body as Legitimacy Operator

#### Archival Discovery & Filological Reconstruction
In 2018, archaeologist Celine Wawruschka discovered a unique colored woodcut in the storage of the Museum Retz (Lower Austria)—published on March 28, 2019 in *Der Standard*. This discovery brought to light the unique surviving copy of the first edition of *Europa sub forma puellae*. Cut in Augsburg in 1534 by Jobst Dennecker based on a design by Tyrolean poet and courtier Johannes Putsch (1516–1542), it was later copied in Paris in 1537 by Christian Wechel as *Europa prima pars Terrae in forma virginis* and popularized as *Europa regina*.

```
          GEOGRAPHIC ANATOMY OF EUROPA REGINA (Putsch, 1534)

                [ CROWN: Spain (Habsburg Royal Crown) ]
                                 │
                                 ▼
                [ HEART: Bohemia (Hortus Conclusus) ]
                                 │
            ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
            ▼                                         ▼
   [ RIGHT ARM: Italy ]                     [ LEFT ARM: Denmark ]
   Holding Sicily (Orb)                     Holding Scepter
            │                                         │
            └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                 ▼
                   [ CHEST: Germania (Breastplate) ]
                                 │
                                 ▼
                   [ SKIRT: Hungary, Poland, ]
                   [ Lithuania, Greece, Moscovy ]
                                 │
                                 ▼
                   [ FEET: Constantinople & Prussia ]
```

#### Anatomical Mapping & Visual Analysis
Putsch maps continental geography directly onto a crowned female royal body:
* **Head/Crown:** Spain (representing the Habsburg royal crown).
* **Heart:** Bohemia, encircled by mountains, cited by Schmale (2000) as a Marian *hortus conclusus* (enclosed garden).
* **Right Arm:** Italy, holding Sicily as an imperial globe (*orb*).
* **Left Arm:** Denmark, holding the royal scepter.
* **Chest:** Germania, rendered as an armored breastplate through a dense network of place-names.
* **Skirt:** Covering Hungary, Poland, Lithuania, Greece, and Moscovy.
* **Feet:** Resting upon Constantinople and Prussia.

#### The *Europa Lamentans* Mechanism & Habsburg Political Dedication
The woodcut was published alongside Putsch's Latin poem *Europa lamentans* (*Lamentatio Europae*), in which the continent-queen speaks in her own voice, lamenting the princely wars tearing her body apart. Putsch’s work carries an explicit political dedication: dedicated primarily to King Ferdinand I of Habsburg (and by extension Emperor Charles V). Meurer (2008) notes the delicate political architecture: while the map's prose dedicates the work to King Ferdinand, the poem subtly holds Emperor Charles V's imperial ambitions responsible for the devastation of Italy.

Applying Ernst Kantorowicz’s *dignitas* model reveals a fundamental departure from the king's body: while the King’s mystical *dignitas* cannot suffer or bleed, the Queen’s *dignitas* is introduced from its origin as a **wounded body**.

#### "So What?" — The Wounded Body as Legitimacy Operator
The queen's gendered grief is not decorative sentimentality; it is an active **operator of legal legitimacy**. The princely wars tearing the continent are waged by male rulers; by giving voice to her suffering through a lamenting woman, the Habsburg sovereign (Ferdinand I) positions his royal rule not as the cause of violence, but as the sole pacifying remedy. Sovereign authority legitimates itself by presenting its power as the solution to the very wounds inflicted upon the continental female body.

#### Political Inversion
In the 1598 satirical print *Het Spaens Europa* (analyzed by Nocentelli, 2023), this weeping body is inverted: Queen Elizabeth I of England occupies the entire continental body, transforming the peripheral British shoulder into the crowned head. The allegory stops weeping and assumes active imperial rule, demonstrating how counter-allegories restructure spatial anatomy to contest Habsburg hegemony.

### 6.3 Case Study II: The Pathology of *Justitia's* Blindfold — Bruegel (1559) vs. Hirsch (1938)

#### Historical Genealogy of the Blindfold
Reconstructing the verified genealogy of *Justitia's* blindfold reveals a non-linear trajectory:
1. **Origin as Satirical Stigma (1494–1543):** Sebastian Brant’s *Narrenschiff* (1494) shows a fool placing a blindfold on Justice—blindness as a trick, stigma, or gag (*mordaça*). Hans Gieng’s 1543 fountain in Bern shows a blindfolded Justice presiding over closed-eyed rulers (Pope, Emperor, Sultan).
2. **Positive Shift to Impartiality (1582):** Andrea Alciati’s *Opera omnia* reinterprets the central judge's blindfold as positive impartiality (flanked by handless counselors).
3. **Baroque Resistance (1618):** Cesare Ripa’s *Iconologia* rejects the blindfold, insisting *Giustitia* must possess "eyes of keenest sight" (*occhi di acutissima vista*).

```
GENEALOGY OF JUSTITIA'S BLINDFOLD
 Satirical Stigma / Gag ──► Positive Impartiality ──► Baroque Resistance ──► Selective Racial Blindness
 (Brant 1494; Gieng 1543)    (Alciati 1582)          (Ripa 1618)           (Capers 2006; Hirsch 1938)
```

#### Comparative Case Analysis: Bruegel (1559) vs. Hirsch (1938)

* **Pieter Bruegel the Elder (1559, *Justitia* drawing / Galle engraving for Cock's *In de Vier Winden*):**
  * *Visual Analysis:* On a central pedestal, a blindfolded *Justitia* holds a sword and scales. She presides over a panorama of mechanical judicial torture: decapitation, gallows, torture wheels, and waterboarding via funnel.
  * *Penal Theory Inscription:* The marginal Latin distich engraved by Galle reads:
    > *"Scopus legis est aut ut eum quem punit emendet, aut poena eius caeteros meliores reddet aut sublatis malis caeteri securiores vivant"*
    > (The end of the law is either to correct him who is punished, or to make others better by his punishment, or that, bad men being removed, the rest may live more secure).
  * *Iconological Reading:* Bruegel contrasts Seneca and Grotius's rationalized, tripartite penal theory with horrifying visual cruelty. The blindfold operates in its satirical regime: blindness is not judicial impartiality, but the institutional refusal to see the atrocities committed in Law's name.

* **Stefan Hirsch (1938, *Justice as Protector and Avenger*, Aiken Federal Courthouse Mural):**
  * *Visual Analysis:* Painted for the New Deal Treasury Section of Fine Arts, Hirsch deliberately depicted an **unblinded** Justice with bare feet and national colors, raising one hand to protect and the other to punish.
  * *Court Reaction & Judicial Violence:* Federal Judge Myers and spectators denounced the mural as a "monstrosity," claiming the unblinded female figure resembled a non-white "mulatto" woman. The tribunal covered the mural with a heavy velvet curtain.
  * *Poetic Synthesis:* Langston Hughes’s 1938 poem *Justice* (cited by Capers) captures this pathology:
    > *"That Justice is a blind goddess / Is a thing to which we blacks are wise. / Her bandage hides two festering sores / That once perhaps were eyes."*

#### Theoretical Synthesis: "Selective and Practiced Blindness"
Synthesizing I. Bennett Capers’ theory (*Michigan Journal of Race & Law*, 2006), *Justitia's* assumed blindness is **"selective and practiced."** The legal system demands blindness when confronting state violence or racialized baselines, but insists on acute vision when policing subordinated populations. The Aiken court's act of covering Hirsch’s unblinded mural with a curtain demonstrates that *Justitia's* blindfold functions to preserve an unstated baseline of whiteness. When Justice is unblinded and perceived as non-white, the institution reimposes blindness externally by veiling the image itself.

### 6.4 Case Study III: Brazilian State Allegories (*1822–1922*) — *Purificação Clássica* and the *Contrato Sexual/Racial Visual*

#### The Arc of Brazilian State Allegories
Analyzing the *Série Documental Brasil (1822–1922)* across major constitutional shifts reveals a visual trajectory:

1. **Early Imperial Indigenist Allegories (1822 onwards):** State iconography utilized tropical, indigenist personifications of America and Brazil (feathered crowns, native flora, indigenous bodies) to symbolize distinct national sovereignty apart from Europe.
2. **Greco-Roman *Purificação Clássica* (Late Empire to First Republic, 1889–1922):** State iconography executed a systematic "classical purification" (*Purificação Clássica*). Indigenous features were erased in favor of whitened, classicized, Greco-Roman draped female effigies (e.g., the *Efígie de Villares* on Republican coins, stamps, and constitutional charters).

#### Cross-Examination: The 1871 *Lei do Ventre Livre* and *Partus Sequitur Ventrem*
Cross-examining emancipation visual prints surrounding the 1871 *Lei do Ventre Livre* (Free Womb Law) against legal statutes reveals the Visual Sexual and Racial Contracts in operation. Official prints depict idealized, classicized female allegories representing a benevolent, maternal State bestowing freedom.

Integrating the doctrine of ***partus sequitur ventrem*** (that the child follows the condition of the mother under chattel slavery) and Jennifer Morgan's theoretical framework demonstrates how state allegories functioned. The 1871 statute co-opted enslaved Black women's reproductive labor by requiring them to raise *ingênuos* (free-born children) while granting slaveholders indemnification or uncompensated labor until the child reached age 21. 

Official state iconography classicalized and whitened civic motherhood, using the immaculate female allegory to mask the continued legal expropriation of Black women's reproductive capacity. The classicized allegory functioned as a visual shield, abstracting state emancipation while erasing the concrete labor, dispossession, and disenfranchisement of Black women.

#### Satirical Counter-Allegories (*Contra-alegoria*)
In response to this official iconography, the Brazilian satirical press (*Revista Illustrada*, *O Malho*, *Careta*) deployed counter-allegories (*Contra-alegoria*). Cartoonists depicted the Republican *República* or *Justiça* not as an immaculate goddess, but as a "wounded," sick, impoverished, or prostituted woman manipulated by oligarchic politicians.

Methodological analysis shows that while these caricatures successfully contested corrupt political authority, they simultaneously reinforced conservative patriarchal stereotypes, using feminine vulnerability, disease, and moral degradation as metaphors for political decay.

### 6.5 Framework Validation & Synthesis
The empirical case studies validate the research framework's core premise: visual montages constructed through strict material controls, isolated ordinal indicators, declared epistemic connectors, and critical historiography reveal the active mechanics of state legal iconocracy.

#### Three Final Research Takeaways
1. **Images are Active Legal Operators:** Female state allegories are not passive decorative illustrations; they execute selective regimes of visibility and authority (*Iconocracia*) that actively construct, mediate, and legitimate legal power prior to textual reading.
2. **Methodological Separation is Essential:** Valid visual legal history requires retiring composite quantitative metrics in favor of isolated ordinal indicators paired with mandatory qualitative verbal inventories (*atributos_iconograficos* / *capta*), preventing false quantitative determinism and preserving scientific auditability.
3. **Visual Hypervisibility Masks Structural Erasure:** The hypervisibility of classicized, whitened female state allegories structurally relies upon the legal exclusion, disenfranchisement, and archival erasure of concrete historical women and racialized populations—a tension that cross-media montage renders fully visible, legible, and auditable.
